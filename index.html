01

Three things that reshape the plan

Silver Week Your first four days are a national holiday

19–23 September 2026 is a rare five-day holiday block — Respect for the Aged Day (Mon 21), a bridge holiday (Tue 22) and the Autumn Equinox (Wed 23). The last one this long was 2015. Kyoto hotel rates run about 53% above a normal midweek night, Osaka 57%.

What I've done about it: parked all four holiday nights in Tokyo, which has the deepest hotel supply in Japan and the smallest percentage uplift, and pushed Hakone, Kyoto and Osaka to 24 September onward when prices fall away. Hakone on 20–23 September would have been the single worst call available — it's Tokyo's default long-weekend escape.

One hard rule: every Nozomi between Tokyo and Hakata is reserved-seat-only from Fri 18 to Wed 23 September. No unreserved carriages at all. You aren't moving cities in that window under this plan, so it doesn't bite you.

Timing Tokyo Game Show is on 21 September

The 30th anniversary edition runs at Makuhari Messe in Chiba, with public days on 19, 20 and 21 September — the day after you land. Tickets are ¥3,000 for anyone 13 or over, sold online only, with nothing available at the venue. For a 17- and 14-year-old this may be the best thing in the entire trip, and it happens on day two or not at all.

Be realistic about it: public days draw well over 100,000 people and it's a holiday Monday. Go early, agree a meeting point, and treat it as a half-day rather than a full one.

Deadline You are 26 days out — book flights and Tokyo hotels first

The optimal booking window for this route is 6–14 weeks ahead; under four weeks typically costs A$200–500 more per person. Waiting will not help you. The specific pressure point is that QF79 is the only nonstop leaving Melbourne on Sunday 20 September, so four seats in the same fare bucket are a finite resource.

Shinkansen reservations open exactly 30 days ahead at 10:00 JST — so your Odawara→Kyoto seats for 25 September go on sale tomorrow, 26 August, at 11:00 AEST.

Flights: the Osaka ending decides this

Start with what doesn't exist, because a lot of stale advice online says otherwise. There is no Qantas Melbourne–Haneda flight (it ended in November 2023). Jetstar doesn't fly Melbourne–Japan at all — its Japan network is Brisbane and Cairns only. And JAL's Melbourne–Narita service is three times a week (Mon/Wed/Sat) until 24 October, so there is no JAL flight on Sunday 20 September.

That leaves one nonstop out, and a real choice about how you come home.

My call

Price the Singapore Airlines multi-city first — Melbourne→Narita on the 20th, Kansai→Melbourne on the 29th. Against the Qantas return it saves roughly A$1,400 in airfare across four people and about A$660 in shinkansen tickets you'd otherwise burn getting back to Narita. That's around A$2,000, or a very good Hakone night plus USJ Express Passes.

An open jaw prices at roughly the same level as a plain Tokyo return on any of the one-stop carriers, because the fares build as two half-returns. On Qantas or JAL it doesn't — neither flies to Kansai.

Take Qantas instead if Tokyo Game Show is non-negotiable. QF79 gets you to the hotel by about 21:30 on the 20th and hands you a full day on the 21st. Singapore lands at 07:35 that morning, which still leaves a workable afternoon at Makuhari after an overnight flight — just a tired one. Cathay's 15:30 arrival does not.

What reviewers actually say

  • JAL's 787 is the best economy seat on the route — 18.8" wide, 33–34" pitch, against the Qantas A330's 17"/31". Its weakness is the entertainment system, repeatedly described as overdue for a refresh. Worth knowing: JL773 does fly Narita→Melbourne on Tuesday 29 September (18:40), so if you go the Qantas route you could still come home on the better aircraft.
  • Singapore and Cathay are both Skytrax 5-star; Qantas is not. On this comparison you are paying more for Qantas to get the nonstop and the schedule, not for a better cabin.
  • Skip Scoot. Its headline fare looks A$300 cheaper, but with no checked bag, no meals, no seatback screens and even power outlets charged as an extra, a family of four lands within A$100–200 of Cathay — who include two bags each and feed you. Reviewers on the long-haul 787 are blunt about it.
  • Ignore the A$870 fares on the Chinese carriers. Those itineraries run 25–35 hours each way. On a nine-night trip you'd spend three days of it in transit to save A$300 a head.
03

Rail: don't buy the JR Pass

This is the biggest money mistake available to you, and almost every generic Japan guide will push you into it. The 7-day national pass is ¥50,000 per person. On a Tokyo–Hakone–Kyoto–Osaka run you would extract about ¥39,000 of JR value — you lose roughly ¥11,000 each, near enough to A$390 across the family.

Four reasons it fails here rather than in general: your trip is ten days and the pass covers seven; the pass excludes Nozomi without a supplement; nothing in Hakone is JR (the mountain railway, cable car, ropeway and pirate ship are all Odakyu, so you buy a Hakone Freepass either way); and the pass only breaks even once you add a long run like Hiroshima or Kanazawa, which you aren't doing.

One thing to be clear-eyed about: on Japanese railways a child is 6–11. Your 14- and 17-year-old both pay full adult fare on every train, and count as adults for hotel occupancy too.

Three things that will make the trip noticeably easier

  • Forward the big suitcases from Tokyo to Kyoto. About ¥2,500 a case, arranged at the hotel front desk. It is next-day, not same-day, so hand them over on the morning of Thursday 24th to have them waiting when you reach Kyoto on Friday evening. It means you do Hakone — mountain railway, cable car, ropeway, boat, a lot of steps — carrying daypacks only.
  • Put Suica in Apple Wallet before you fly. Wallet → + → Transit Card → Suica. No deposit, no expiry, tops up with your Australian card from anywhere. Physical cards take cash only. Android phones bought in Australia lack the FeliCa chip, so anyone on Android buys a plastic card at Narita.
  • Book the shinkansen on SmartEX as each date opens — 30 days ahead at 10:00 JST, which is 11:00 AEST. Four seats together on a peak train is a harder ask than one. Ask your bank to enable 3D Secure first; overseas cards get declined on Japanese ticketing sites constantly without it.

Sat 26 SepKyoto: shrine, market, samurai

Fushimi Inari before 08:00. It's free and open around the clock, and the hour is the whole trick — by mid-morning the tunnel of torii is shoulder-to-shoulder. Most people turn back at Yotsutsuji, about 45 minutes up, where the view over the city is. The full summit loop is two to three hours if anyone's keen.

Lunch at Nishiki Market. One local rule worth passing on to the teenagers: don't eat while walking. It reads as genuinely rude here — eat at the stall.

Afternoon at the Samurai & Ninja Museum, four minutes from Nishiki — a 60-minute English tour with armour to try on, katana practice and shuriken throwing. ¥2,400 adult, ¥1,900 student. Book ahead; the groups are small. Kimono rental runs ¥3,190–4,180 for a basic plan if anyone wants it, and late September is still warm enough to ask for a lighter yukata.

Gion in the evening. Stay on the main streets — entering the private alleys carries a ¥10,000 fine and it is enforced, and don't photograph geiko or maiko without asking.

Check in around Namba or Umeda, then Dōtonbori after dark, which costs nothing and does more for a teenager's impression of Japan than most paid attractions. Mon 28 SepUniversal Studios Japan

A non-holiday Monday is the best day this trip can give you — after Silver Week, off the weekend. Adult tickets run ¥8,900–10,900 depending on the date band, and at USJ 12 and over is an adult, so that's four full-price tickets. Buy online before you go — the ticket booths at the gate stopped operating in May 2025.

Two things people get wrong. First, Super Nintendo World usually needs a separate timed-entry area ticket, free through the USJ app but only once you're inside the park, and slots vanish early on busy days. Donkey Kong Country sits inside the same area and uses the same entry. Second, not every Express Pass includes Nintendo — read the inclusions line by line. Express Pass 7 is the sensible value point at ¥11,500–26,000; with standby queues running 120–200 minutes in Halloween season, it's the difference between three rides and eight.

One age rule that matters to you: Halloween Horror Nights runs from 11 September, and two of the mazes are a hard ban for anyone 14 and under — no exceptions, even with a parent present. The street zombies and shows are fine.

Where to stay

The structural problem in Japan is that most hotel rooms sleep two or three, and occupancy limits are legally enforced under the Fire Defense Law — you cannot simply put four people in a triple. So the shortlist below is built around rooms that genuinely sleep four, with two-room alternatives where they don't.

The chain doing this best is MIMARU, whose apartment-style rooms have real kitchens, separate bath and toilet, and 35–45 m² of floor — which with teenagers is the difference between a holiday and a stand-off. Why Ikebukuro over Shinjuku or Shibuya

Shibuya is the place you want to visit and a painful place to sleep four — genuine quad rooms there are close to non-existent and you'd be booking two rooms at a premium. Shinjuku has the best transport in the city, including the direct Romancecar to Hakone, but MIMARU charges roughly 40% more there for an identical apartment.

Ikebukuro has the highest concentration of what your teenagers came for — the world's largest Animate, Pokémon Center Mega Tokyo, Sunshine City, Namja Town — for the lowest price, and it's 16 minutes to Tokyo Station on the Yamanote. Shinjuku is 8 minutes away on the same line when you need the Romancecar on the 24th.

Hakone, honestly

A$600–800 buys a good onsen hotel with a buffet dinner, which for a 14- and 17-year-old is a considerably better night than a twelve-course kaiseki they'll poke at. It does not buy a kaiseki ryokan with a private open-air onsen in the room — those start around ¥25,000 per person. Yumoto Fujiya is my pick despite being at the top of the range: it's a three-minute walk from the station the Romancecar terminates at, it has reservable private hot-spring baths, and it runs several restaurants so nobody has to eat something they don't want to.

One line item that won't appear in your booking total: lodging tax, collected at check-in. Kyoto raised its rate in March 2026 to ¥1,000 per person per night at this price band; Osaka is ¥500; Tokyo ¥200. Budget ¥8,000–12,000 across the trip.

Two pieces of timing luck

  • You're inside the old tax-free system. Until 31 October 2026, spend ¥5,000 or more at one shop in one day, show your passport, and the tax comes off at the register. From 1 November Japan switches to paying full price and claiming a refund at the airport. You miss that by five weeks.
  • The last week of September is the good weather. Expect humid 27–30°C days easing through the week, warm nights, and no autumn colour yet — Kyoto's foliage is a mid-November event. The 2026 typhoon season is forecast above average overall, but September approaches to Honshu are forecast below normal. Rail suspensions are announced the evening before, flights 24–48 hours out. Keep the Kyoto Railway Museum, teamLab and Kaiyukan in your pocket as wet-day swaps, and buy insurance that covers weather disruption.

Money

Cards work almost everywhere in these four cities now, but carry ¥20,000–30,000 a head for small restaurants, shrines and markets. Seven Bank ATMs inside 7-Eleven are the default — English menus, foreign cards, everywhere. Always choose to be charged in yen, never in AUD; dynamic currency conversion at the terminal costs 3–7%. And ask your bank to enable 3D Secure before you go — SmartEX and several Japanese ticketing sites decline overseas cards without it.

Things that have already sailed

Worth knowing so you don't spend a week chasing them. Ghibli Museum September tickets released on 10 August and are gone; the October release is 10 September if you'd shift plans, and cancellations occasionally surface on Lawson. Ghibli Park September tickets went on sale 10 July — every ticket must be reserved, no exceptions. The Nintendo Museum in Uji ran its September lottery back in June; your only route now is the leftover and cancellation sale, checkable daily at museum-tickets.nintendo.com, so create the Nintendo Accounts with verified mobile numbers now rather than while a ticket sits in a cart. Don't build a day around it.

And one that's simply out: the Mario Kart street go-karting needs drivers aged 18 or over holding an International Driving Permit, and most operators don't carry under-18 passengers. It doesn't work for a 17- and 14-year-old.
